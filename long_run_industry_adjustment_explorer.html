Constant-cost
Industry expansion does not change firms' input costs. Long-run price returns to its original level.
Increasing-cost
Industry expansion raises input costs. The new long-run equilibrium has a higher price.
Decreasing-cost
Industry expansion lowers input costs. The new long-run equilibrium has a lower price.

What changes in the long run?

Constant-cost industry
More firms enter and industry supply expands enough to return price to the original level. Quantity increases.
Increasing-cost industry
Entry expands supply, but expanding the industry raises input costs. Long-run price ends higher than before.
Decreasing-cost industry
Industry growth creates lower input costs or external economies. Long-run price ends lower than before.

Important: LRS describes the industry's long-run relationship between price and total industry output. It is not the supply curve of an individual firm.
